Italy's investor demand reached 279 applications from January 1 to October 13, 2026, more than in any previous full year. The year-to-date total passed 2025's record of 229 applications.

Over the 12 months ending September 2026, investors filed 340 applications, 2.7 times the 125 filed in 2024. Applicants have shown a stronger preference for the lower-cost innovative startup route, and US nationals account for more than half of this year's filings.

These figures come from Italy's Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy (MIMIT). [IMI Daily reported](https://www.imidaily.com/intelligence/imi-exclusive-demand-for-italys-investor-visa-has-nearly-tripled-in-two-years/) that the ministry released monthly data after a freedom of information request from the Italian Investment Alliance. The dataset covers applications since February 2018 and breaks them down by nationality, investment route, and province.

## Americans account for most 2026 applications

Americans filed 160 of the 279 applications received by October 13, 2026. The American share of applications rose from 34% in 2024 to 44% in 2025 and 57% in 2026 year to date.

The 160 applications from Americans this year exceed the 125 applications filed by all nationalities in 2024. They also exceed the 92 applications filed by US citizens from 2018 through 2024.

## Italy's program is residence, not citizenship

The Italian investor visa is often marketed as a golden visa. The investment is a route to residence, not immediate citizenship.

An approved applicant receives a certificate of no impediment and can use it to request an investor visa at an Italian consulate.

The visa holder receives a two-year residence permit, renewable in three-year periods while the qualifying investment is maintained.

The program sets no minimum time in Italy, according to the information supplied with the MIMIT data, as reported by IMI Daily.

## Portugal's HQA route is not an investment visa

[Portugal's highly qualified activity (HQA) route](https://www.portugalhqacitizenship.com/guides/what-is-hqa-visa) is not a statutory investment visa. The label describes a residence route based on highly qualified work, research, teaching, or a qualifying cultural activity under Portuguese immigration law.

That makes HQA different from Italy's investor visa.

Italy has defined investment routes with statutory minimums. The [ministry page](https://www.mimit.gov.it/it/impresa/competitivita-e-nuove-imprese/investor-visa) includes:

- **€250,000** for an innovative startup.
- **€500,000** for an Italian company.
- **€2 million** for Italian government bonds.
- **€1 million** for a philanthropic donation in listed public-interest fields.

Portugal's HQA route instead centers on the applicant carrying out a qualifying activity in Portugal. The HQA guide says the relevant articles of the immigration law do not set an investment amount.

## Panama grants permanent residence through investment

Panama's Qualified Investor Visa grants permanent residence in exchange for a qualifying investment. Naturalization is a later, separate application.

The [Henley & Partners Panama overview](https://www.henleyglobal.com/residence-investment/panama) describes real estate, listed securities, and bank deposits as investment options. It says the investment must be held for at least five years to maintain permanent residence.

The qualifying amounts on that page are:

- **US$300,000** for newbuild or first-sale real estate.
- **US$500,000** for secondary-market real estate.
- **US$500,000** for a stock-exchange investment.
- **US$500,000** for a fixed-term deposit with a state-owned bank, or **US$750,000** with a private bank.

Italy's program first grants a temporary residence permit. Panama's Qualified Investor route issues permanent residence, subject to review by the immigration service.

## Argentina announced citizenship by investment

Argentina announced two investment routes on October 2, 2026. The [government notice](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/luis-caputo-anuncio-la-puesta-en-marcha-del-programa-de-ciudadania-por-inversion-de) sets them out as:

- **US$350,000** as a non-refundable contribution to the national treasury.
- **US$800,000** as a subscription to a public bond created for the program.

The same notice says the program will be open to receive applications during the fourth quarter of 2026. [Reuters reported the announcement the same day.](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/argentina-announces-citizenship-by-investment-program-2026-10-02/)

The announced Argentine program differs from Italy's investor visa.

Italy's innovative startup route offers residence through investment. Citizenship, where it is available, comes later through a separate naturalization process.

Argentina's announcement describes an investment route to citizenship, subject to assessment and a final decision by the immigration authority.

The announcement does not publish processing times or approval rates. It sets the opening for the fourth quarter of 2026 and does not confirm that filings were already being accepted on October 2.

The [Henley & Partners Argentina overview](https://www.henleyglobal.com/citizenship-investment/argentina) summarizes the same contribution, the same bond, and the same expected opening window.

## These programs lead to different results

The programs above are not interchangeable.

Italy's investor visa links a defined qualifying investment to residence.

Portugal's HQA route relies on qualifying work, research, teaching, or a cultural activity. It is not the same thing as a residence permit that depends on a statutory investment.

Panama's Qualified Investor Visa grants permanent residence through a qualifying investment. Naturalization requires a separate process.

Argentina has announced a citizenship by investment program. The government said applications would open in the fourth quarter of 2026.
